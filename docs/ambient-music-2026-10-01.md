# Newer Game ambient music

The supplied recording is integrated locally as `music/ambient.mp3`, with its original bytes preserved. Embedded metadata identifies **Iron Cthulhu Apocalypse** and **Hallucinations (Lovecraftian Dark Ambient Hour)**. The artist is credited in the game and README. The source is 59,447,130 bytes, approximately 61 minutes and 41 seconds; [asset attribution and SHA-256](../music/CREDITS.txt).

## Behavior and interpretation

Newer Game plays a quiet background bed. Only safe movement triggers occasional small swells; combat or nearby enemies keep the background quiet. This interpretation was stated to the owner after asking whether combat should retain the background or fade it out completely; no answer to that optional preference question had arrived during implementation. Complete combat silence can be selected by changing the gameplay eligibility condition, without changing the streaming transport.

- Baseline gain is 0.075. A swell adds at most 0.025, approximately 2.5 dB, before the existing master gain. It rises over four seconds and eases down over nine seconds. The first swell waits 10–25 seconds of safe movement; later swells wait 35–90 seconds of safe movement. Stopping or becoming unsafe cancels a swell and smoothly returns to baseline.
- Two streamed decks choose bounded random 1,200-second sections. The last 12 seconds overlap the next section, whose offset is kept well away from the preceding one. Each deck's section clock follows actual media playback, including its overlap, so paused time and buffering do not count as music heard. The whole recording does not loop from beginning to end.
- Classic games, title demos, loading/disconnection, menus/console, pause, death, intermission and hidden tabs pause playback and silence the music bus. Visibility changes also pause immediately when host frames are suspended.
- The existing sound-volume slider (`volume`) controls the master gain. `bgmvolume` controls music once, and `bgmvolume 0` pauses it. `nosound` also suppresses it. This ambience deliberately follows the master sound slider; the older CD-audio module retains its existing routing and behavior.

## Existing architecture and ownership

`host.js` calls `S_UpdateAmbientMusic` beside the existing sound/CD update. `s_ambientgame.js` reads existing game state, while `s_ambientmusic.js` owns only its two media elements, their sources/gains, the ambient bus and the visibility listener. It shares `S_GetAudioContext()` and routes into `S_GetMasterGain()`. No second audio context or whole-track AudioBuffer is created. Media playback volume remains one, avoiding double application of `bgmvolume`.

Playback is initialized lazily during eligible Newer gameplay; demos and classic games do not create music elements or request the recording. The file remains outside `newer/`, so `tools/build_newer_pak.py` cannot add a duplicate hour-long stream to startup pack loading. Static hosting should support byte ranges for efficient random seeking. Deploy `music/ambient.mp3` and its credits file alongside the game. A host without range support may need to transfer the whole compressed file before distant seeks become available.

The local policy reads authoritative `sv.edicts`: living monsters within 512 units prevent swells even when off-screen or behind walls. Monsters targeting the player within 1,024 units count as engaged. Enemy players are considered in deathmatch. Attack input, player weapon sounds, damage tint and health/armour loss extend a ten-second combat cooldown. Free/dead entities are ignored. A world-model change resets previous damage history. Remote servers expose less AI state, so the fallback uses known live enemy models and excludes explicit death poses; it cannot know hidden monsters omitted by the remote server.

`S_UnlockAudio` retries blocked media only on real user input. A rejected audio-context resume stays suspended for the next gesture rather than generating an unhandled promise rejection. Stop/shutdown use the existing sound lifecycle; shutdown pauses and releases both media resources, removes callbacks/listeners and disconnects owned nodes before the shared context closes. Generation checks prevent old play promises from restoring stopped state.

The incoming fade advances only while its media is ready and its playback clock progresses. An incoming stall cannot promote a silent deck merely because wall time passed. Expired outgoing sections are stopped; a failed, blocked or persistently stalled incoming stream falls back to a fresh bounded section on the working deck with a fade-in. Unsupported/missing media stays silent and exposes its error through `S_GetAmbientMusicStatus`, without disrupting game sound or retrying a failed play promise on every host frame. Small timing differences of one host audio-update interval can occur at section boundaries.

The Credits screen keeps its original 320×200 layout and source-code link/hotspot. Existing section spacing is compacted to add “Ambient music / Iron Cthulhu Apocalypse” at rows 152/160. Source remains at 172/180.

## Trying the increment

Hard-refresh the normal page, choose **Newer Game**, and provide a normal keyboard/mouse/touch gesture to unlock browser sound. Quiet music should start; walk in a cleared area long enough to hear a slight swell. Firing, taking damage or approaching living enemies prevents swells. Choosing **New Game** or setting `r_hdr 0` stops the music. Open **Credits** to see the artist. `bgmvolume 0` disables the music; the normal sound-volume slider also governs its output.

For a repeatable trial, open `/tests/ambient_music_trial.html` on the local server. Its shortened-slot control exercises real MP3 seeks/fades through the production host policy. The movement control sends actual game commands; the nearby-enemy control creates a temporary authoritative, non-rendered monster fixture only in the trial. It is removed by the trial control or map reload. Production code never spawns test enemies or shortens sections.

The browser trial also observed an actual safe-walking swell; its precise envelope and limits are additionally proved by focused tests. This is working local verification. It does not qualify every remote mod/browser/audio device, a forty-minute uninterrupted session or an FPS benchmark. Browser diagnostics included generic Chromium `UnknownError` entries without a useful application stack, while the music decoded and progressed with no transport error or assertion failure; no browser-console-clean claim is made. The owner has not yet judged the final loudness, but authorised committing and pushing this verified increment. No release or durable deferred job was submitted.
