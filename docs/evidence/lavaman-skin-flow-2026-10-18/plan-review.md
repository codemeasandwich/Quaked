# Lava Man skin flow: independent plan review

Date: 2026-10-18. Reviewer: independent `water_review` helper. Implementation owner: parent coordinator and `electric_spatial_tests` helper. This document is an **unstaged planning record**, written while the separate staged distribution gate runs. No runtime source, index, tests, build output, owned model, or supplied image was changed by this review.

## Owner requirement and current defect

The owner requirement first clarified movement and stretching of the **skin features over the unchanged native body**, rather than a surrounding shimmer substituting for that movement. Subsequent explicit owner steering separately approved **additional heat shimmer from the hot creature**, while retaining the mandatory skin flow, and asked for color/intensity matching intense natural HDR lava. This later authority is the current scope. It supersedes the older CPU mesh-deformation interpretation. Existing bounded flame and smoke attachments remain part of the established Lava Man effect. Additional heat must reuse the existing composite heat-haze mechanism; it must not introduce a second compositor pass, scene-copy texture, or full-screen effect.

The inspected `src/newer/render/r_lavaman.js` still computes displaced positions and Jacobian-adjusted normals into private buffers, substitutes those geometry attributes, recomputes bounds, and supplies a cosmetic floor-shadow pose. That is the implementation to replace. Retained commit `ba3a183e` (`test: require Lava Man texture flow on native geometry`) establishes the revised independent test contracts; it changes tests, not this runtime behavior. Its presence does not establish a working implementation or a passing trial.

## Source trace and smallest compatible change

`R_DrawAliasModel` in `src/engine/render/gl_mesh.js` selects the canonical native/custom material and native or interpolated position/normal attributes before calling `R_LavaManApply`. It can select the canonical material again on the next draw. Main and neighboring level-view scenes call `R_LavaManSeen` after the native draw/transform, with their existing visual-time input. The effect therefore belongs in the current Lava Man adapter, after native interpolation and within its established scene-token lifecycle.

Use `R_CloneAliasMaterial` from `r_newerskins.js` to make at most one owned skin material per admitted actor. That helper preserves the canonical compiler, texture references, native-skin binding, and renderer-specific cache-key composition. Wrap that compiler rather than replacing the Newer alias shader. Invoke it with the clone as its material receiver, preserve every canonical uniform/output, then apply one shared UV-flow function. Keep the canonical base material and borrowed image textures unchanged. Rebind the owned material after the native draw selects its base, and replace/dispose the old clone when the canonical base/catalogue, model/header, mesh, or required topology changes.

The canonical shader already uses `vMapUv` for pigment, normal-map sampling and derivatives, emission, gloss/sheen, and the height-shadow context. All those channels must share the **same warped skin coordinate and derivatives**. Applying flow only to the diffuse map would let painted features slide independently of their relief. Conversely, a height context initialized with warped coordinates must not have those coordinates warped a second time inside `qrShadowHeight`; retain a single sampling-domain transformation. Preserve existing G-buffer albedo, normal, and height-mask semantics, receiver behavior, native-skin binding, and Classic material selection.

Do not write position, normal, UV, index, blend-lead, native animation, or bounding-volume data. Do not allocate replacement body attributes or recompute bounds for skin flow. `R_LavaManPositions` should return `null` so existing shadow code uses the canonical native/interpolated pose. Continue deriving the existing eight fire and twenty-four smoke instances from the native triangles and outward normals; their source shader bodies, borrowed assets, and ownership budgets remain the existing ones.

## Additional heat and native lava intensity

The actual existing heat implementation is `r_post_liquids.js` plus the composite shader in `r_post_shaders.js`, configured through `r_heathaze`; there is no separate `r_heathaze.js` module in the inspected tree. It selects four nearby lava-pool regions and ray-intersects their rising heat boxes, clipped by opaque scene depth. It excludes held receivers and sets effective heat to zero underwater. Add a separate bounded collection of at most eight current-main-view Lava Man heat sources to this same pass, without consuming, reordering, or evicting the four pool slots. The producer `R_LavaManHeatSources` must expose only eligible actors actually seen in the current main-view scene token. Cached level-view ghosts, unseen previous-frame actors, Classic renders, unsupported model/skin/material state, custom-off actors, and released/cleared entries must not contribute lingering heat. Heat-source production must not depend on optional flame atlas readiness.

The actor boxes must derive from canonical native current-pose world bounds without changing geometry or mutating its stored bounds. Bound the spatial extent and shader loop, clear counts every update, and clip each ray segment to scene depth so nearer walls/objects occlude heat behind them. Retain the held-receiver exclusion and underwater/off gates. Classic must retain its unmodified appearance, and ghost rendering must not overwrite main-view heat state. Heat is an independent on/off contribution: it must never become evidence that the body's pigment itself moves.

Reuse the existing natural-lava registration and pulse: `R_RegisterGlow`, `LAVA_BOOST = 6`, and the existing `pulseLava` recipe, rather than adding an unrelated brightness timer or arbitrary independent multiplier. Register owned actor materials only, preserve native/Classic tint and shared base state, avoid compounding a previous boost when cloning/rebinding, and let disposal remove the owned material from glow registries. The literal GPU comparison must include an actual native `*lava1` material through the same HDR pipeline at a controlled pulse time. Compare relevant bright lava texels/body highlights and pre-tonemap intensity as well as final color; a shared numeric multiplier alone does not demonstrate that two different albedo textures look like the same intense natural lava. Record any art-dependent visual tuning and seek owner acceptance of the final trial.

## UV domain, clocks, ownership, and failure contracts

The retained audit requires a conservative native UV-chart domain, exposed through `uLavaDomain` and native `uLavaAtlasSize` (300 by 194). Its linear, non-mipmapped RGBA data carries chart identity and clearance. A bounded warp must remain within its original chart; uncertain/overlapping/outside regions need zero permitted displacement. This avoids pulling unrelated atlas islands or background across the head and body. Shared topology data must be immutable during animation, shared between actors, and disposed exactly once after its last consumer or clear. This is generated controller data, not a new painted source image. Keep its dimensions tied to the native atlas rather than the resolution of the replacement art.

`uLavaTime` and `uLavaPhase` are independent, stable uniform objects per actor. Time comes from the existing caller's visual time, never wall-clock accumulation or render order. Same-time redraw and rewind must reproduce the same values; another actor or neighboring scene must not advance them. Actor phase must remain stable through native pose changes. Shader cache keys must identify identical shader source across actors and times, with no material-version/recompile churn for ordinary clock or pose updates.

Retain exact model hash and native skin-zero admission, ready-material gating, `r_newer_enemies`, Newer mode, active-ghost checks, max-eight admission, and existing main-actor priority over cached ghost reservations. Pending/failed art, unsupported identity, invalid input, or capacity overflow must leave the usable canonical material/body in place. A temporary Classic pass hides Newer effects and retains the Newer clock/material allocation; real clear/release retires it. Restore the base only if the mesh still uses the owned clone. Never overwrite a successor's material/attributes or dispose native geometry, canonical materials, or shared image textures. Dispose owned clones and effect layers on their established lifecycle paths; recover optional fire assets independently of body-skin availability.

## Required verification before delivery claims

Run the retained public-interface suites against the actual owned Rogue model, not a fabricated mesh. The CPU contracts include all ninety native poses at three times; position/normal/UV/index object identities and bytes; unchanged bounds; native/interpolated shadow and interrupted-blend leads; distinct actor uniforms; same-time/rewind behavior; material cache stability; catalogue replacement; actual Classic save/restore; scene ownership; optional-asset failure/recovery; native triangle attachments; and the eight-actor allocation cap. The audit also independently checks conservative UV-chart identity/clearance and generated-domain ownership. Retain original failures and any fixture corrections; a test-only contract commit is not runtime proof.

Actual WebGL/GPU evidence is required after the current heavy gate releases its slot. At a fixed native pose/camera/light, render distinct skin times through the actual alias material and G-buffer path. Prove visible pigment movement inside independently identified head/body masks while depth, silhouette, and native geometry stay unchanged. Inspect actual normal and height-mask outputs so aligned relief is demonstrated, not assumed from shader strings. First disable additional heat and separate existing fire/smoke from the body measurements; a patterned background must then remain unchanged outside those existing layers. Separately enable actor heat and prove a localized visible distortion, unchanged four-pool behavior, correct near-wall occlusion, held-receiver protection, and zero contribution underwater, in Classic, when disabled, for ghosts, and after scene/actor cleanup. Include the actual native `*lava1` HDR comparison described above. Confirm main and neighboring views, pause/rewind, Classic toggling, and cleanup without shader/GL errors. Record which checks are controlled fixtures versus gameplay trials.
