## Native behavior

Living faces blink for 150 ms, scheduled every 3–6 game-time seconds using the
existing injectable RNG. Blinks do not reset gaze or expression. Pause freezes
game time; power activation cancels a current blink, and power expiry schedules
a fresh interval. Quad and Pentagram, separately or together, suppress blinking.
Death overrides stale power flags with `focused_determined` and `eyeState=dead`;
actual positive-health respawn resets normally. World, client-clear, native
edict-array, demo-file and reversed-time boundaries reset transient state.

`IT_SUIT` independently selects the diving equipment. Water reads the verified
local player's native `air_finished` and server time while eyes are submerged
(`waterlevel===3`). The bundled QuakeC program uses a 12-second air reserve;
its own suit logic replenishes that reserve. Consequently, a working suit
keeps the immediate W1 underwater indicator while it replenishes air.
The owner’s later timing amendment makes entry show W1 immediately, then
`min(10,floor(clamp(percent,0,100)/10)+1)` while submerged. W10 appears at
90% air used, leaving the final 10% (1.2 seconds of the native reserve).
On surfacing, the displayed stage drains backward by one every 50 ms of game
time, reaching W0 in at most 500 ms. Pause freezes draining; re-entry cancels it
and follows the current native air reserve. Death, respawn, new-world/clock
reset, noclip and unavailable air state clear it, including a hidden HUD. The adapter never writes health, damage, air,
inventory or power-up timers.

Remote/demo packets do not contain an air deadline or exact head-submersion
state. They report `waterKnown=false` and clear water rather than inventing an
air timer or consulting an unrelated local server. Their suit and other power
flags still follow their own client data.
