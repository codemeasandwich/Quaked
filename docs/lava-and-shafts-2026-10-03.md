# Stronger shafts and local lava illumination

The owner requested four times the default light-shaft intensity, plus glowing lava that lights adjacent surfaces. This increment reuses the existing HDR, bloom, light-selection and deferred surface-lighting paths. It changes `src/gl_post.js`; the original lava material and turbulent-UV path in `src/gl_rsurf.js` already supplies the required unlit source texture and remains unchanged.

## Shaft control contract

`r_pillars` remains `0.5` by default. The original sun-scattering calculation is held at that nominal setting inside the volume buffer, and one shared compositing multiplier applies to all of that buffer's sources:

`shaft gain = r_volumetric * 4 * max(0, r_pillars) / 0.5`

At `r_volumetric 1`, slider settings `0`, `0.125`, `0.5`, and `1` therefore give gains `0`, `1`, `4`, and `8`. Sun rays, ordinary point-light shafts and the flashlight beam pass through this multiplier once. The slider now controls all three. Zero skips the volumetric pass. The gain scales linear shaft radiance before exposure and display tone mapping; it does not promise four times the final screen RGB value. Direct surface lighting, sunlight, exposure, shadow step counts and bloom settings are unchanged by this gain. Lighting off and Classic bypass it.

## Lava source and receiver contract

Native turbulent textures retain their fullbright palette texels in the original map texture. `R_LiquidSurfaceMaterial` uses that original picture in an unlit `MeshBasicMaterial`. Existing `R_RegisterGlow`, `R_PostBegin` and `pulseLava` now apply a lava-only base gain of `4` with the existing bounded pulse (`0.79..1.21`), yielding `3.16..4.84` linear material intensity. Bright lava texels consequently reach HDR bloom instead of staying below its threshold. Native and lighting-off materials return to intensity `1`. Lava remains opaque, with its original depth-writing, UV turbulence, geometry and physical contents.

The previous surface emitter builder collapsed a whole subdivided lava face into one pool-centre source. Its capped power and fixed sixty-unit surface falloff could leave distant pool edges and nearby platforms almost unlit. The existing emitter builder now clusters actual lava polygon patches into its existing 192-unit cells. Opposing face normals have separate keys, preventing their offsets from averaging into a hidden source inside the pool. Generated sources retain the existing power cap, ten-unit outward offset, source visibility policy, 500-source map bound and eight-selected-light frame bound. The source texture name is recorded on each derived surface light for diagnostics; map entities and dynamic lights are unchanged.

The shared `pointSurfaceIncident` shader uses `max(60, sourceRadius)` for the area-source falloff scale. Current ordinary map lamps have radius `28` and dynamic lights radius `40`, so both keep their previous sixty-unit falloff. Larger existing surface emitters can illuminate their own edges; deferred opaque receivers and confirmed reflection receivers reuse that same helper. Screen-space visibility still limits occlusion to available depth information. No extra light system, geometry pass, collision change or map-light entity was introduced.
